The Speed-to-Lead Playbook

A homeowner who hits your voicemail calls the next business on the list. This is the setup that gets you into the conversation first — the three pieces behind the Husky Roofing numbers, written so you can build them yourself.

Summit Solutions10-minute read

Speed-to-lead is the single biggest lever in home services, and almost nobody pulls it. Studies going back years put it bluntly: contacting a web or phone lead within the first few minutes dramatically out-converts waiting even an hour. Homeowners rarely call one company — they call three and hire whoever answers. If you're on a roof, under a sink, or driving between jobs, that's usually not you.

The fix isn't hiring someone to stare at the phone. It's three automations that run whether or not anyone's free. Here's each one.

1. Missed-call text-back that actually converts

The instant a call goes unanswered, fire a text from your business number. This is the highest-ROI automation you can turn on — it reopens a lead that would otherwise be gone in 90 seconds. Three rules make the copy convert:

  • Lead with the apology and the name. It reads human, not robotic.
  • Give one clear next step — a booking link — not an open-ended "how can we help?"
  • Keep it short. One or two sentences. Long texts feel like marketing.

Paste these in and swap the brackets for your business:

Business hours

Hi, this is [Name] at [Business] — sorry we just missed your call! What's going on with your [roof/plumbing/HVAC]? Or grab the first open time for a free estimate here: [booking link]

After hours

Hi, it's [Business] — thanks for calling! We're wrapped up for the day but I don't want you waiting. Tell me what you need and we'll get you on the schedule, or book a free visit here: [booking link]

No reply after ~10 minutes

Still here if you need us! Most folks just grab a time that works and we come take a look — no charge for the estimate: [booking link]

Compliance note

Because you're texting from a business, US carriers require A2P 10DLC registration before messages reliably deliver. Skip it and your texts quietly get filtered. We cover the whole process in the A2P 10DLC guide.

2. A booking page leads fill out themselves

The text-back only works if the link goes somewhere that books the job without a phone call. You want a page that shows your real availability and drops confirmed appointments straight onto a calendar. Set it up like this:

  1. Define the appointment type. For most trades that's a "Free Estimate" or "Inspection," 30–60 minutes, with buffer time so back-to-backs don't collide.
  2. Set true availability. Connect the calendar your team already lives in so the page never offers a slot you can't make.
  3. Ask only what you need. Name, phone, address, and a one-line "what's going on." Every extra field drops completion.
  4. Confirm on the spot. The moment they book, the slot's held and everyone gets notified (next section).

Tools that do this well out of the box include your CRM's native scheduler (GoHighLevel, HubSpot), or standalone options like Calendly and Google Appointment Schedules. The key is that the booking link in your text-back points here — not to a "call us" page.

3. Confirmations and reminders that kill no-shows

Booked isn't the same as showed. A short reminder sequence is what turns appointments into estimates actually given. Wire up four messages:

  • Instant confirmation — fires on booking: "You're confirmed for [day] at [time]. We'll text a reminder the day before. Reply here if anything changes."
  • 24-hour reminder — "Looking forward to seeing you tomorrow at [time] for your free [service] estimate. Anything we should know before we arrive?"
  • 2-hour reminder — "[Name] is on the way to you around [time] today. See you soon!"
  • Office notification — a text and email to your team on every booking so nothing depends on someone refreshing a calendar.

Add a self-service reschedule link to the confirmation. A lead who can move the appointment in two taps won't ghost you — they'll rebook.

On deliverability

Reminders only work if they arrive. For the email side — confirmations, estimates, follow-ups — sending from a properly authenticated domain matters more than most owners realize. See the email deliverability guide.

Putting it together

Turn on all three and a missed call becomes: text-back in 30 seconds → self-booked estimate → confirmation and reminders → a visit that actually happens. That's the whole loop Husky Roofing runs, and it booked them $43K in new jobs in their first six weeks — without anyone chasing leads by hand.
