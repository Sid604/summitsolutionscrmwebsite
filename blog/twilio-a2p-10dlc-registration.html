A2P 10DLC registration, explained for contractors

If your business texts quietly stop landing, this is almost always why. Here's what A2P 10DLC is, why carriers require it, and how to register without losing a weekend to it.

Compliance8-minute read

You set up missed-call text-back, send a few tests, and they work. Then a week later customers say they never got your text. Nothing errored. The messages just… vanished. Nine times out of ten, the cause is the same: you're texting from a business without being registered for A2P 10DLC, and US carriers are filtering you.

What A2P 10DLC actually means

A2P stands for Application-to-Person — any text sent by software rather than a human thumbing a phone. 10DLC means a 10-Digit Long Code, i.e. a standard local phone number (as opposed to a short code or toll-free number). Put together, A2P 10DLC is the US carrier framework for sending automated business texts from a normal local number.

The mobile carriers (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile) built this system to cut down on spam and fraud. The trade: legitimate businesses register who they are and what they'll send, and in return their messages deliver reliably. Skip registration and your throughput is throttled or your messages are blocked outright — usually without any error you'd notice.

Where Twilio fits

Twilio is the messaging infrastructure a huge share of texting tools run on under the hood — including many CRMs. "Registering with Twilio" really means submitting your A2P details through Twilio to The Campaign Registry (TCR), the central body carriers check against. If your CRM uses a different provider, the same concepts apply; only the dashboard changes.

The two things you register

  1. Your Brand. This is your business identity: legal name, EIN, address, website, and contact. Carriers verify it against public records, so the details must match your EIN registration exactly.
  2. Your Campaign. This is the use case for your texts — for home services, usually "Customer Care" or "Mixed." You describe what you send (appointment confirmations, reminders, follow-ups), provide sample messages, and show how customers opt in and opt out.

Step by step

  1. Gather your business details. Exact legal name, EIN, registered address, and website URL. Mismatches are the #1 cause of rejection.
  2. Register the Brand in your messaging provider's console (Twilio, or your CRM's A2P section). A one-time vetting fee applies.
  3. Create the Campaign. Pick the use case, write 2–3 sample messages, and describe your opt-in. Include your business name in messages and a "Reply STOP to opt out" line.
  4. Publish your consent language. Carriers want to see where customers agree to be texted — a line on your booking form and a note in your privacy policy. Ours spells out SMS consent and STOP/HELP handling as an example.
  5. Wait for approval. Brand vetting is often quick; campaign approval can take a few business days. Once approved, link your phone number(s) to the campaign.
  6. Send a real test to a phone on each major carrier and confirm delivery before you rely on it.

Getting approved on the first try

  • Match your EIN records exactly — legal name and address, character for character.
  • Make opt-in obvious. "By submitting this form you agree to receive texts about your appointment. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • Honor STOP and HELP automatically. Most platforms do this natively; confirm it's on.
  • Don't send before approval. Early unregistered traffic can get your number flagged.
Short version

A2P 10DLC isn't optional and it isn't a scam fee — it's the toll for your business texts to actually arrive. Register the brand, register the campaign, publish clear consent language, and your missed-call text-back and reminders deliver like they should.

Once you're registered, the next question is email: your confirmations and estimates need to land too. That's a different set of rules — see the email deliverability guide.
